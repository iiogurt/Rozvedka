How an entity's neighbours are linked among themselves: one row and one column per neighbour, a mark where a source states a link between two of them, ordered so that communities form blocks.

How this is ordered

The neighbours are the entity's direct links in the chosen sources. At most {{ size }} are kept: those with most links to the other neighbours first. Countries are left out unless asked for. Communities come from the Louvain method on the links among them (Blondel et al. 2008): a node moves to the neighbouring community that raises modularity most, and communities are merged and moved again until nothing changes. A link stated by several sources weighs more. A community is named after its most linked member and is drawn as a block on the diagonal; its rows run from most to fewest links inside the matrix. A mark is coloured by the strongest source that states the link, and a dot inside means several sources state it. Every mark opens the link's evidence on Find a connection. Neighbours linked to none of the others are listed below the matrix. A community is a pattern in the stated links, not an organisation.
